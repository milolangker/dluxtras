Validation: how many wavelengths?¤

recommend_wavelength_sampling(K, N) (see Spectra) turns the number of constrainable spectral modes K and the detected photon count N into a number of wavelengths to propagate:

  • Hermite: n_coarse = max(3, 2K) coarse nodes on a fixed fine grid;
  • Exact: n_exact = ceil(23.5 K^0.553 (N/1e9)^0.208) wavelength bins.

Both rules were fitted to JWST/NIRCam models. This page re-derives them on generic optics and synthetic filters, using only dluxtras, dLux, JAX and NumPy, and checks whether either rule ever asks for too few wavelengths.

Reproduce with:

python validation/wavelength_sampling.py          # ~6 min
python validation/wavelength_sampling.py --quick  # ~30 s, 1 pupil, 2 filters, 2 stamps

Run on 2026-10-16, Apple M5 Pro CPU, jax 0.11.2, dLux 0.15.1, NumPy 2.5.4 (full run: 375 s).

Setup¤

  • Optics. dl.AngularOpticalSystem, D = 1 m, 256-pixel pupil, oversample 3, summed to detector pixels. Two pupils:
    • clear: an unobscured, unaberrated circle;
    • obscured: a 20% central obscuration, four spider vanes 1% of D wide, and 50 nm RMS of random Zernike aberration (Noll 4–11, about λ_c/20, Strehl ~0.9).

  • Filters. Four synthetic smooth top-hats centred on 1 µm, with tanh edges and a 20% linear throughput tilt (like a QE slope). Their fractional bandwidths (at 1% of peak, as fractional_bandwidth measures them) and λ_c/D in detector pixels match the four NIRCam filters of the original study:

    filter Δλ/λ_c λ_c/D [px] mimics
    N3 0.030 2.14 F212N
    M17 0.166 1.86 F182M
    W29 0.291 2.20 F444W
    W53 0.530 1.62 F322W2

  • Stamps of 32, 64, 128 and 256 px, i.e. half-widths R_half of 7–80 λ_c/D. The source sits at a sub-pixel offset of (0.3, −0.2) px with a flat incident spectrum.

  • Photons N = 1e8, 1e9 and 1e10, where N is the detected photon count in the stamp (the truth image sum).

Method¤

For each (pupil, filter, stamp):

  1. Truth. 600 exact monochromatic PSFs on the spectrum_args(nwavels=600) grid, summed with CombinedBasisSpectrum.weights.
  2. K. The Fisher matrix in incident-photons-per-bin coordinates, with Poisson variance equal to the image, is built once at unit flux from the cached PSFs. K(N) then follows analytically, since the criterion 1/(|a0| sqrt(d_i)) scales as N^-1/2; it's counted with constrainable_modes (threshold 0.1).
  3. Model error. Δχ² = N Σ_p (I_model − I_truth)² / I_truth, with both images divided by the truth sum.
  4. Minimum n_coarse. Hermite images on the same 600-bin spectrum, so only the interpolation error enters. Every integer n_coarse from 3 upwards is scanned, and the minimum is the smallest value beyond which every scanned value has Δχ² < 1. (Δχ²(n_coarse) isn't monotonic: node alignment makes it ripple, in 18 of 32 cases.)
  5. Minimum n_exact. Exact images from spectrum_args(nwavels=n) with their own binning, so this is the quadrature error. Found on a grid, then bisected to the integer.
  6. Rules. Both rules are evaluated at K(N) with recommend_wavelength_sampling. Each prediction's own image is then tested: a rule undershoots if Δχ² ≥ 1 there.

The script also runs these consistency checks:

  • the truth equals dl.Telescope(optics, HermitePointSource(...), detector).model() to 1e-14;
  • the cached Hermite images match HermitePointSource(n_coarse=...) (Δχ² to 1e-11);
  • the Fisher matrix equals spectral_eigenmodes to 4e-15, with the same K at the library's photon count;
  • 600 vs 900 bins in the hardest case (W53, 256 px) gives Δχ² ≤ 1.2e-3 at N = 1e10.

Conclusion¤

Neither rule undershot in any of the 96 cases (Δχ² < 1 at the prediction in 96/96 for both). The generic setup reproduces the NIRCam study closely: K is typically 5–20% above the NIRCam values for the matching filter and stamp (by 0–1 for the narrowest filter), and min n_coarse / K spans 0.75–1.50 (NIRCam: 0.87–1.78). Both rules are confirmed, and both are conservative:

  • Hermite, max(3, 2K): at least 1.33× the minimum (median 1.9×). The tightest cases are at N = 1e10. max(3, ceil(1.45 K)) would already have sufficed here (NIRCam needed 1.70), so c = 2 leaves about 20–40% headroom for setups less benign than these.
  • Exact, 23.5 K^0.553 (N/1e9)^0.208: at least 1.31× the minimum, but with a median of 2.3× and up to 4.7×. The overshoot grows with stamp size. At fixed filter and N, the minimum n_exact barely depends on the stamp (W29 at 1e10: 91–134 from 32 to 256 px), while K roughly triples, so the K^0.553 term over-predicts for large stamps.

Caveats:

  • The speedup estimate is biased towards Hermite. recommend_wavelength_sampling estimates the speedup as n_exact / (2.5 n_coarse) with the conservative n_exact rule. Against the measured minimum n_exact, it predicts ≥ 1.5× when the true figure is below 1.5× in 36 of 96 cases, mostly narrow filters or small K at high N (e.g. N3 at 32 px: estimated 1.8–4.1×, true 0.6–1.2×). The recommendation is still safe (both rules are), but it can pick the slower option. The jvp_cost of 2.5 was not re-measured here.
  • The filters are smooth. The quadrature error, and so n_exact, depends on how sharp the throughput is. Real filters with steeper edges or ripples need more bins. At N = 1e10, NIRCam's F322W2 needed 150–200 (on a coarse grid), against 103–145 for W53 here. Check n_exact with a real throughput curve before relying on a margin as small as 1.3×.
  • The tested range is narrow. These results cover one source position, a flat source spectrum, Poisson noise only, 600 fine bins, and N = 1e8–1e10, the range the rules were fitted on.
